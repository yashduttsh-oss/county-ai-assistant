## The three county-brief questions (Owen County)
1. Where can AI stretch small public and nonprofit staffs in grants, records,
   and communications?
2. Which main-street firms would join a training cohort?
3. Could the public library serve as the county's AI access point?

## Illustrative framing (sample narrative)
Small public bodies and nonprofits in rural counties typically carry heavy
administrative load relative to headcount. Three task areas recur:
- **Grants:** drafting and tailoring grant applications, and tracking
  reporting deadlines, consume disproportionate staff time.
- **Records:** responding to public-records requests and organizing meeting
  minutes and permits is often manual.
- **Communications:** routine public notices, newsletters, and social posts
  compete with core duties.

Main-street firms — retail, hospitality, and professional services clustered
around the county seat — most often cite marketing, scheduling, and customer
correspondence as the tasks that could benefit from assisted tooling, provided
training is short, local, and low-cost.

A public library can act as a neutral, trusted access point for AI literacy
because it already offers public computing, has predictable hours, and carries
no sales motive. Constraints to check include staff capacity, meeting-room
availability, and broadband headroom.
